## Newest this session (2026-10-21): "Scan a packaged food" — photo-to-catalog with admin review (built, locally verified, not yet deployed)

User feedback relayed verbatim: *"there are many processed foods people are consuming which are bought from the retail outlet, for some this makes a significant chunk of their daily diet. it is practically very hard to add every brand across the world to the menu list. can we take input from the user via a photo of the package with the nutrition information and convert it and store it for future use. Should we directly add the item information directly to the master database and make it available to the universe or just store it as the user's favourite or list and add it to the database once after the admin approval?"* — then, as a follow-up: *"yes. can we take the micronutrient from the ingredients list. can we also do the same for nutrition supplements"*. Scoped with Aravinth via a direct choice between packaged foods first / supplements first / both together — he chose **packaged foods first**; supplements have not been started and shouldn't be, without him confirming he's ready to move on to that.

**Facts-only extraction, same discipline as `api/read-report.js`:** a new `packaged-food-scan` kind was added to the existing `api/ai-helpers.js` dispatcher (no new serverless function — `api/` stays at 10 files, 2 under Vercel's Hobby-plan 12-function cap). Claude reads the photographed label and extracts only what's printed: product name, brand, serving size text, and 20 nutrient fields (calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber, and 15 micronutrients), each either a real printed number or `null` — never an estimate. On Aravinth's specific micronutrient question: an ingredient that's merely *named* on the label (e.g. "contains added Vitamin B12") without a printed quantity is **never** converted into a guessed number — it's surfaced separately as an `unquantifiedAdditives` note instead, and the ingredients list itself is also captured and shown. All extracted amounts are pinned to the units `lib/nutrition-core.js` already expects (g/mg/mcg per nutrient) with an explicit instruction to leave a field null rather than guess at a unit conversion it isn't confident about.

**No new table — ownership/review columns added directly to the existing `foods` table** (`add_packaged_food_scanning.sql`): `owner_user_id` (null = shared, already-approved catalog — every existing row defaults to this, so nothing about the current catalog changes), `review_status` (`pending`/`approved`/`rejected`), `source` (`catalog`/`packaged_scan`), plus `brand`, `serving_size_text`, `ingredients_text`, `unquantified_additives`, `photo_storage_path`, and submission/review timestamps. This was a deliberate simplification from an earlier, more complex "separate submissions table" sketch — it means promoting a scan to the shared catalog is a single `UPDATE ... SET owner_user_id = null, review_status = 'approved'`, with no data copy and no `diet_entries` migration, since `diet_entries.food_id` keeps pointing at the same row regardless of ownership.

**RLS: a real leak risk was found and fixed, not just asserted — verified against a real local Postgres 16 instance.** `foods` has presumably always carried a fully-public `SELECT using (true)` policy (it's been an open, admin-curated catalog) — Postgres OR's same-type RLS policies together, so a new "see your own pending items" policy does **nothing** if that old permissive one is still active. Built a stand-in `auth.users`/`auth.uid()`/`authenticated`-role local Postgres setup, seeded `foods` with that exact kind of pre-existing permissive policy, ran the real migration against it, and then simulated two separate users: confirmed Bob really could see Alice's private pending scan while the old policy remained (a real, demonstrated leak), and confirmed it closed — while the shared catalog stayed visible — once that old policy was dropped. Also confirmed the insert policy correctly rejects an attempt to insert pre-approved, someone-else-owned, or `source='catalog'` rows from the browser, and that a service-role-style approval UPDATE correctly makes a row visible to everyone afterward. **Because this can't be verified from here against Aravinth's live database, `add_packaged_food_scanning.sql`'s STEP 0 is a read-only check that lists every existing policy on `foods` — he needs to actually look at that output and manually drop any non-owner-aware `SELECT` policy it finds before the privacy boundary is real; the file's own comments spell this out.**

**Self-identified bug fix (not user-reported):** `api/telegram-webhook.js` queries `foods` with the service-role key, which bypasses RLS entirely — so its two full-table reads (`loadFoodCatalog()`, `loadFoodsFull()`) needed their own explicit `owner_user_id.is.null` filter added, or Telegram's meal-matching and gap-suggestion logic could have surfaced another user's private pending scan. Re-ran the full 37-check Telegram activity-logging test harness afterward to confirm zero regressions.

**Website (`index.html`):** a "📦 Scan a packaged food" button added next to the existing "Can't find a food? Let us know" row on the diet-log "add food" card. Taking a photo (resized to 1600px, higher than the 1024px used for meal photos, for label-text legibility) shows a confirm-before-save review panel — every extracted field in an editable input, exactly like the existing meal-photo-confirmation flow — plus the ingredients text and any unquantified additives shown as notes. ## 2026-10-20: production-wide AI outage (Oct 10–20) — `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` had expired — diagnosed and fixed, confirmed resolved

Aravinth and his wife had been trying to log meals via Telegram for several days and kept hitting errors: a raw `"invalid x-api-key"` message in one case (via free-text logging outside the guided flow, which routes through the dietitian-chat fallback when intent classification itself fails silently), and a generic `"Something went wrong on my end -- try again in a moment."` in others (via the guided Log-a-meal flow's item-extraction step, where a thrown API error isn't caught specially and falls through to the handler's top-level catch).

**Diagnosed by cloning the live repo fresh and tracing both error strings to their exact source** (this session's earlier local checkout was gone — the cloud workspace had been recycled — so re-cloned `github.com/aravinthvc/Nutrigap-app` to inspect the real deployed code rather than guessing from memory). Found that `lib/dietitian-agent.js`'s `callDietitianModel()` passes Anthropic's raw `error.message` straight back to the Telegram user as `err.userMessage` when the API call fails — that's the literal source of the `"invalid x-api-key"` leak. The guided-flow path (`extractMealItems()` in `api/telegram-webhook.js`) throws on the same failure but isn't specially caught, so it surfaces as the handler's generic fallback message instead. **Both are symptoms of one root cause, not two separate bugs.**

**Root cause confirmed in the Vercel dashboard and Claude Console (Aravinth checked live, shared screenshots):** the `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set in Vercel (Production/Preview/Development, all added Sep 10) pointed at a Claude Console API key that **expired Oct 10, 2026** — a 30-day default expiry set when the key was originally created, with nothing in the app or Vercel to warn ahead of time. Last-used date on the expired key was Oct 8, lining up exactly with when the errors started.

**Scope: this wasn't Telegram-only.** `insights.js`, `read-report.js`, `ai-helpers.js` (photo-log, recipe-ideas, explain-marker), and `dietitian-chat.js` on the **website** all read the same single `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` — so every AI-powered feature on both channels was almost certainly down for the same ~10 days, not just Telegram meal logging. The only reason "Meal ideas" still worked for Aravinth in one screenshot is that its default (no-named-nutrient) path is catalog-based and doesn't call Claude at all.

**Fix, step 1 — new key:** Aravinth generated a new Claude Console API key with **expiry set to Never** (deliberately, not the default — see reasoning below) and updated all three Vercel environment slots (Production/Preview/Development).

**Fix, step 2 — a real gotcha that caused a false "still broken" report:** saving a new value for an environment variable in Vercel does **not** by itself redeploy anything — serverless functions keep running on whatever was baked into the *last actual deployment* until a new one is triggered. Aravinth updated the key and tested immediately, still got the same errors, and reported it as unfixed. Checked the Vercel Deployments list directly and confirmed the latest Production deployment was still from **Oct 9** (`876bbef`) — nothing had redeployed since the key update. Fix: Aravinth used the `•••` menu on that deployment → **Redeploy**, which picks up the current environment variables without needing a code change.

**Confirmed working** — Aravinth reported "WORKING NOW" after the redeploy. Also flagged a low-balance warning on the Anthropic account at the same time the new key was created, worth keeping an eye on (auto-reload or manual top-ups) so this doesn't recur via a different failure mode.

**Why "Never" expiry was the right call here, not a security shortcut:** Claude Console's own UI warns that long-lived keys are a leak risk, but that risk is about the key ending up somewhere public (a repo, client-side code, chat). This key only ever lives in Vercel's encrypted env vars, server-side — never in `index.html` or the repo. Meanwhile there's no monitoring/alerting in this stack to catch a silent expiry before it causes a multi-day outage, and Aravinth works via copy-paste with no automated key-rotation process. Given that tradeoff, an expiring key is strictly the bigger operational risk for this setup.

**Not yet done, worth considering later (not asked for yet):** the raw-error-leak in `lib/dietitian-agent.js` (`err.userMessage = message` straight from Anthropic's response) means *any* future Anthropic-side failure — not just an expired key — would again show raw internal error text directly to end users instead of a friendly message. Worth a small fix to give that path the same kind of honest-but-generic error message `extractMealItems()`'s failure path effectively gets by accident. Flagged here so a future session doesn't have to re-diagnose this if it's ever asked for.

**Lesson for future deploy troubleshooting:** when Aravinth reports "I updated X in Vercel but it's still broken," check the Deployments tab's timestamps before re-diagnosing the original problem — an env-var change with no redeploy is a very easy false negative, and looks identical to "the fix didn't work" from the user's side.

## Earlier this session (2026-10-08): viral marketing features — referral/invite program (website + Telegram) + shareable weekly progress cards

Aravinth: "Lets make features that can increase viral marketing opportunity." **Schema (`add_referrals.sql`):** one `profiles.referral_code` column (unique, generated client/bot-side on first use — 7 characters, an alphabet that excludes 0/O/1/I to avoid visual confusion if read aloud or retyped) plus a new `referrals` table (`referrer_user_id`, `referred_user_id` unique — this is what makes crediting idempotent, `source` constrained to `website`/`telegram`). RLS is deliberately narrow: a user can SELECT referrals where they're the referrer (to show "N friends joined"), but there's no insert/update/delete policy for the browser at all — every write goes through a service-role path that has already resolved and verified the code → referrer relationship first.

**New, `api/record-referral.js`** — the website's half of that service-role bridge: the browser's own Supabase session can only ever read its OWN profile row under RLS, never look up someone else's by their referral_code, so resolving a code to its owner has to happen server-side. Called once, right after a new signup. Self-referral and an unresolvable code both come back as "not credited" rather than erroring a signup already in progress; a retried/duplicate call never double-credits (the `referrals` table's unique constraint on `referred_user_id`, backed up by the endpoint's own pre-check).

**Website (`index.html`):**
- `?ref=CODE` on the URL is captured into `localStorage` and the URL is cleaned immediately on page load — survives a Google OAuth redirect or an email-confirmation gap, since there's no account yet to credit the referral to.

### Decision: no race/ethnicity input in the energy-needs model

Aravinth asked how the model should vary energy/nutrient needs by race and by age group. Researched this (see chat for sources) before answering: NutriGap's `computeTargets()` already varies energy needs by age (direct term in the Mifflin-St Jeor BMR formula) and sex, and varies micronutrient DRI targets by a 6-bracket age table — all pre-existing, no gap there. Race/ethnicity deliberately stays **out** of the energy formula: real but small measured RMR differences between some racial groups persist even after adjusting for body composition (~5% in one well-cited study), but the effect is too weak and inconsistent for mainstream dietetics to have adopted a race-specific version of Mifflin-St Jeor (the equation's own original validation didn't even report its sample's racial makeup) — so NutriGap's current race-agnostic approach matches standard practice, not a shortfall. Where ethnicity genuinely matters more is a separate question: WHO recommends lower BMI cutoffs for Asian populations for overweight/obesity risk classification (≥23/≥27.5 vs. the standard ≥25/≥30), since Asians carry more visceral fat and face elevated diabetes risk at a lower BMI — relevant to BFB's mostly-Indian customer base if a "healthy weight band" feature is ever added, but NutriGap doesn't compute or display BMI at all today, so this isn't fixing an existing gap. No code changed from this — it's a recorded decision so a future session doesn't redo the research or assume race belongs in the kcal formula.
